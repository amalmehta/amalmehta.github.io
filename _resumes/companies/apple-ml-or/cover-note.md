# Cover note: Apple, Machine Learning/Operations Research Engineer (Machine Learning and AI), Cupertino

I like machine learning most when it changes what happens in a real system, and a factory floor is about as real as it gets.

For four years at PhysicsAI, a DARPA-funded program, I treated synthetic-data generation as an optimization problem: RL policies searched an image generator's settings, and the objective was a detector's accuracy on real drone footage, not a proxy. Real labels were scarce, so I also combined synthetic and real data with domain adaptation I built from scratch. Before that, at IBM Research, I joined unstructured contracts with invoices to catch spend leakage, work published at INFORMS.

Since then I've built a batched simulator of a real drone to test what-if conditions such as payload, wind and motor wear, and post-trained small models with SFT, GRPO and DPO, including a code-repair agent. I build most of this with Claude Code.

I'd like to bring that to Apple's manufacturing, where a better model shows up directly as throughput and cost.
